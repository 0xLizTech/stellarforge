## Summary

One paragraph. What is this proposal, in plain language? A reader should be able
to decide whether to keep reading from this section alone.

## Motivation

Why should we do this? What problem does it solve, for whom, and what happens if
we do nothing? Link the issue, discussion, or incident that prompted it.

## Guide-level explanation

Explain the proposal as if teaching it to another protocol engineer: new
concepts, terminology, and how the change appears from the outside (contract
interface, SDK surface, operational runbook). Include worked examples where they
clarify more than prose.

## Reference-level explanation

The technical detail. Precisely describe the interface, types, storage entries,
events, authorization, and edge cases. This section should be detailed enough
that an implementer who has never spoken to the authors could build it, and
enough that a reviewer can find the security-relevant seams.

## Drawbacks

Why might we *not* want this? Be honest: complexity, cost, migration risk,
maintenance burden, or loss of optionality.

## Rationale and alternatives

What other designs were considered, and why is this one preferred? If the
proposal departs from an existing ADR, say so and explain why.

## Prior art

How do other protocols or systems solve this? What did they learn?

## Unresolved questions

What is deliberately left open at this stage, and who or what resolves it?
Address these before requesting `Accepted` status.

## Security and upgrade considerations

What could go wrong? Enumerate the trust assumptions the change adds or removes,
the failure modes, and — if the change touches deployed contracts — how existing
deployments are upgraded or migrated.

## Adoption plan

How does this land? Break the work into steps (contract change, SDK change,
docs, deployment). Note what is out of scope for the first iteration.
